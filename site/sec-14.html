14 Open Problems and Research Agenda

Every open problem in this section is derived from a specific object this survey has already named, a failed or unverified edge in Table 26, a blocked or unexercised route in Section 11, an unsupported or undocumented cell in Table 24, or a resolved conflict file from this survey's own audit. None is a generic future-work item stated without that anchor. Section 14.1 collects the specific objects the remaining subsections key against. Section 14.6 closes with a table linking each stated problem back to its originating edge, route, cell, or conflict, and to one concrete research direction.

14.1 Failed and Unverified Contract Edges

Two edge-level facts anchor most of what follows. First, SpikingJelly's default \(v_{\mathrm{reset}}=\mathrm{None}\) blocks both of SpikingJelly's own exporters at their first edge, through two different mechanisms, an explicit code-level guard in the Lava exporter and the absence of any field for subtractive reset in the NIR schema (Table 26, rows 1 and 2; Section 11.5, Section 10.3). Second, this survey located no documented route for subtractive reset through custom Loihi 2 microcode outside the public Lava and NetX artifacts, an edge recorded as undocumented rather than blocked because Loihi 2's neuron engine is documented as fully microcode-programmable and an inference of impossibility from this absence would be unwarranted (Section 11.5).

Table 24 supplies the platform-level version of the same gaps. Threshold and reset is unsupported on the audited routes to Loihi 2 and both SpiNNaker generations. Plasticity and state persistence is undocumented for SpiNNaker 2's audited NIR import path and unsupported for Speck and Xylo. Synaptic dynamics and delay is unsupported for the audited Speck route. Each of these cells names a specific, versioned gap in a specific audited configuration, not a general ceiling on the underlying silicon, exactly the distinction Section 5.6.3 already insists on for a software-programmable platform's own capability record.

14.2 Algorithm-Hardware Codesign for Heterogeneous Schedules

Section 8.4.3 deferred a specific question to this subsection, the tradeoff between NeuroFlex's column-level and a coarser layer-level granularity for mixing ANN and SNN execution modes within one inference. This survey's own audit of PASCAL, QAC, MT-SNN, and Temporal Flexibility sharpens that question. None of the four sources demonstrates a static, heterogeneous, per-layer schedule executing on physical or cycle-simulated hardware. PASCAL's and APEX's RTL results cover only a uniform-timestep overhead check, not the mixed-horizon schedule itself (Section 8.3.4, Section 13.8); QAC's pipeline-stall mechanism, a downstream layer waiting for an upstream layer to finish a different number of steps, remains a qualitative warning, not a measured result (Section 4.1, Section 8.3.4).

The open problem is therefore precise rather than diffuse. A mixed-timestep method that assigns a different static horizon to each layer has no audited evidence, at any evidence class, that the resulting heterogeneous schedule executes correctly and efficiently on a barrier-synchronized, asynchronous, or software-programmable target from Section 5. A concrete research direction is to synthesize or fabricate a scheduler that resolves QAC's own named stall mechanism for a stated execution family, and to report the result against at least the E4 hardware-model boundary this survey already applies to PASCAL and APEX, extending it to the mixed-horizon case those two papers do not cover. A second, related direction follows Section 8.4.3's column-level and layer-level distinction directly, testing whether a finer execution granularity changes the stall behavior QAC describes only at the coarser layer level.

14.3 Portable Reset and Precision Semantics

Reset semantics survive a software or compilation boundary in a minority of Table 26's eleven typed edges, and numeric precision is quantized at nearly every boundary, at widths that differ from edge to edge (Table 26; Section 10.6). NIR's declarative schema does not by itself certify identical integration order, spike timing, reset timing, or numerical quantization across two backends that accept the same graph (Section 10.3).

No interchange format or compiler chain audited in this survey preserves reset semantics and precision jointly across a training framework to hardware chain without either transformation or outright rejection along the way. This is an open problem in the schema itself, not only in any one framework's implementation of it. A concrete research direction is an interchange extension, whether to NIR or to a successor schema, that encodes reset mode as a first-class field alongside a per-quantity precision declaration naming weights, thresholds, and state separately, verified by demonstrating that at least two of Table 26's audited edges preserve the extended field where they currently transform or reject it. Graph operators and supported operations compound this problem rather than sitting outside it. Every one of Table 24's nine audited platforms transforms an arbitrary input graph before executing it, so a portable semantics proposal that addresses reset and precision alone would still leave the operator-set boundary this survey finds uniformly restrictive.

14.4 Measurement and Reproducibility Gaps

Section 12's measurement checklist names seven fields a comparable deployment report must state, and Section 13's applications evidence documents specific, sourced gaps against each of them (Section 13.9). Two further, more specific reproducibility failures surfaced during this survey's own audit rather than in its secondary literature. PASCAL's published ResNet-18 and CIFAR-10 headline of \(T_{\mathrm{eff}}=3.14\) is reproducible from the paper's own released script only when its 17 listed per-layer timestep values are silently truncated to the 10 energy coefficients a Python zip() call actually consumes, an undocumented cardinality mismatch neither the paper nor its code addresses. MT-SNN's withdrawn ICLR manuscript and its later accepted Frontiers abstract name different temporal-alignment mechanisms, average-and-repeat alignment in the former and a named Scaled Synaptic Current Accumulation mechanism in the latter, so the two records document the same layerwise-allocation lineage without being interchangeable evidence for how alignment is actually implemented.

Both cases point to the same open problem at different scales. A headline number in this literature is not reliably checked against the released artifact that is supposed to produce it, and a method's identity is not reliably stable across a manuscript's own revision history. A concrete research direction is procedural rather than technical, adopting Section 12.6's checklist as an artifact-review requirement before a deployment number is cited as reproduced, and requiring that a paper's released code be executed against its own stated headline before that headline enters a downstream comparison table, exactly the check this survey performed for PASCAL specifically and found to fail silently.

14.5 Access and Maintenance Risk

Three access and maintenance facts recur across this survey's platform and route evidence (Section 10.6). Intel's 2026 archival of the Lava, Lava-DL, and NetX stack freezes every audited edge terminating at Loihi 2 through that stack, including the blocked SpikingJelly-to-Lava route in Table 32, at whatever state it held at the point of archival. Loihi access remains gated to Intel's Neuromorphic Research Community rather than openly available, a condition that governed the NxTF-to-Loihi 1 route while it was an open chain and still governs access to Loihi 2 today. Lynxi's BIDL, Lyngor, and LynSDK toolchain is documented as effectively unavailable outside mainland China. A fourth, structural fact compounds the first three, every platform-capability record behind Table 24 remains provisional pending full-text promotion of its underlying vendor manual (Section 9.4).

The open problem here is distinct from ordinary software rot in reproducible machine learning research. A route this survey classifies as physical today, NxTF to Loihi 1 among them, can become a closed chain within the same toolchain's own generational transition, and a route this survey classifies as blocked can become permanently so once the stack it depended on is archived with no successor, as happened to the Lava-based routes during the audit period this survey itself spans. Reproducing or extending an audited result therefore depends on infrastructure access and continuity that this survey's own evidence shows is neither guaranteed nor evenly distributed across platforms. A concrete research direction is independent, vendor-neutral archival of reference toolchain versions, worked examples, and their exact commit identifiers, tied explicitly to a platform-capability record's own verification status rather than left to a vendor's own retention policy, so that a future audit can distinguish a route that has become genuinely obsolete from one that has merely become harder to access.
