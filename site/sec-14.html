14 Complete Deployment Routes

Section 13.7 closed by distinguishing what a platform can do in principle from what a specific published chain from training framework to silicon has actually done. This section closes that gap for seven named candidate routes, chosen because each pairs a training or conversion framework already introduced in Section 12 with a hardware family already introduced in Section 13, and because each is a route a reader of this literature is likely to assume exists in something like its named form. Each route is traced edge by edge against this survey's generated route index, and a route absent from that index, however plausible its two named endpoints look together, is not treated as a route at all.

14.1 What Counts as a Named Route

A named route in this section is a chain of two or more edges, each typed by the schema Section 12.3 already applies, connecting a training or conversion framework to a physical or intended hardware target. A route's overall state is not asserted independently of its edges. It is read off the platform-capability record that names the specific chain of route identifiers behind it, exactly as Section 10.4 already distinguishes a field's capability status from a route's state. Six states are available for this purpose, exact, approximate, blocked, obsolete, unexercised, and physical, fixed once in this survey's evidence vocabulary and applied here without redefinition. A route can fail at its first edge, in which case every downstream edge is conditional on a hypothetical model that never reaches it, or it can succeed through every edge to a documented physical measurement. Both outcomes are reported in what follows with the same care, because a blocked route is exactly as informative to a practitioner choosing a deployment path as a physical one.

One naming caution applies before any route is traced. The plainest name for a candidate route is not always the exact chain the generated route index records. Where this survey's evidence traces a different, more specific intermediate node than the name a reader might expect, this section states the discrepancy explicitly rather than silently substituting the expected name for the audited one.

14.2 Physical Routes

Four of the seven candidate routes reach a physical target under this survey's audited evidence, and a fifth reaches physical hardware for a different execution mode than the one its name suggests.

SNN Toolbox → NxTF → Loihi 1. SNN Toolbox's Keras, PyTorch, Caffe, and Lasagne-ingested, percentile-normalized, reset-by-subtraction IF conversion bridges into NxTF's Keras-derived compiler through a dedicated interface module, and NxTF compiles the result down through NxSDK's register-level NxCore API onto physical Loihi 1 silicon . Every edge in this chain is E1. The result is, in this survey's audited evidence, the clearest published rate-coded conversion result on physical neuromorphic silicon, an 8.52 percent CIFAR-10 error on a 413,000-neuron, 3-million-parameter network spanning 1,753 cores across 14 chips . The route's threshold-and-reset field is emulated rather than native, recovered only through the two-compartment workaround Section 12.3 already traces, and NxTF's own authors list a native soft-reset primitive as a desired future hardware feature specifically to remove that cost . This chain is also a closed one. NxTF and NxSDK were never ported to Loihi 2, and Intel's own 2022 Neuromorphic Research Community guidance redirects members to Lava for that generation instead . This survey's platform-capability record accordingly assigns the route state physical to this chain specifically for Loihi 1.

SNN Toolbox → sPyNNaker → SpiNNaker 1. The audited chain is more specific than a name built around the generic PyNN interface would suggest. SNN Toolbox's own SpiNNaker output module builds PyNN-based convolutional and dense layers from a parsed model and drives sPyNNaker, PyNN's SpiNNaker-specific backend, directly, rather than passing through the generic, backend-agnostic PyNN interface as an intermediate stage in its own right . sPyNNaker's own SpiNNTools stage then partitions the graph, places it, compresses routing tables to the chip's router budget, and signals synchronized execution start across physical SpiNNaker 1 silicon . An earlier, independent deep-belief-network conversion on a single physical SpiNNaker 1 chip reports 95.01 percent MNIST accuracy at 0.3 watts and roughly 20 milliseconds latency, all measured on silicon, and a later result credits SNN Toolbox's own conversion methodology at 98.20 percent MNIST accuracy on a larger SpiNNaker-103 machine, though the exact code path is not confirmed to be this specific module in every source consulted . As Section 13.3 already states, this route's threshold-and-reset field is unsupported for the audited default configuration, because sPyNNaker's built-in cell types use PyNN-conventional hard-reset semantics and reset by subtraction requires a bespoke C neuron model outside the audited chain. This survey's evidence also locates no CIFAR-10-scale or larger convolutional network run on physical SpiNNaker silicon through any documented path; every physical result located is digit-recognition scale or a small number of convolutional layers, a scale limit distinct from the route's own state.

Sinabs → DynapcnnNetwork → samna → Speck. Sinabs's from_torch.from_model call replaces ReLU activations with subtractive-reset integrate-and-fire layers by default, and its DynapcnnNetwork mapper restructures the result into convolution, spike, and pooling blocks, quantizing weights to 8 bits and membrane state to 16 bits . samna then applies the resulting configuration object to physical Speck silicon and streams event data in real time . Multiple physical results confirm this chain, a dynamic-SNN DVS-Gesture result reporting 81.0 percent accuracy at 9.5 milliwatts for a fixed-rate configuration against 90.0 percent accuracy at 3.8 milliwatts for a dynamic configuration on the same chip, and an official NIR-to-Speck tutorial reporting 90.00 percent on-chip accuracy on N-MNIST . Unlike the two routes above, this chain's threshold-and-reset field is native rather than emulated, because Speck's own hardware default, return_to_zero=False, already implements subtraction and Sinabs's own default reset function matches it without a workaround . The route still transforms other fields on the way, average pooling becomes sum pooling because spikes cannot be meaningfully averaged, a linear layer becomes a 1x1 convolution, and Speck imposes one uniform threshold per spiking layer rather than a per-neuron threshold, so native status for one field does not extend to the graph-operators or precision fields on the same chain.

Rockpool → Xylo. A trained Rockpool computational graph passes to a mapper that extracts a hardware specification and checks it against Xylo's fixed CuBa-LIF architecture through a design-rule check, and a quantization stage then fixes weights to 8 bits and thresholds to 16 bits before XyloSamna or XyloMonitor deploys the result to physical Xylo Audio 2 silicon . A published micro-power keyword-spotting benchmark on the physical XyloAudio 2 development kit, with on-board current monitors sampled at 1280 hertz, is this chain's E1 result . Subtractive reset is preserved as a fixed hardware property on this route rather than a software switch, matching the native threshold-and-reset status Section 13.7's comparison table already records for Xylo. The route also departs from the single-spike-per-timestep assumption most conversion theory relies on, because Xylo's core supports up to 31 events per neuron per step, a capability this survey's audited evidence does not find exploited by the specific keyword-spotting benchmark cited here.

hxtorch → BrainScaleS-2, a physical route to a different target than its name suggests. This chain is not one of the seven originally listed candidate routes, but it belongs in this subsection as a caution against a mistake this survey's own audited evidence makes concrete rather than hypothetical. hxtorch's perceptron primitives execute non-spiking analog matrix multiplication on BrainScaleS-2 in its hagen mode, and a physical measurement exists, latency and energy for a mobile electrocardiogram application measured on the physical system . This measurement applies to the non-spiking analog execution mode alone. It must not be read as a measurement of hxtorch.snn's spiking, leaky-integrate-and-fire execution mode, a distinct route with its own separate, E2, hardware-in-the-loop evidence already developed in Section 6.3 and Section 13.4 . A route can be physical and still not be the route a name suggests it is.

14.3 Approximate and Transformed Routes

QuantizeML → CNN2SNN → Akida. QuantizeML's uniform, symmetric integer quantization output is consumed directly by CNN2SNN's convert() call, which maps the result to Akida's .fbz runtime format, folding batch normalization and activation quantization into a per-neuron static threshold computed once at conversion time . This is a documented, first-party BrainChip pipeline order, and the Akida runtime executes the resulting model on physical AKD1000, AKD1500, or Akida Pico silicon, an E1 chain for the Akida 1.0 generation . This survey nonetheless places the route in this subsection rather than treating it as an unqualified physical instance of rate-coded conversion, because the same evidence that establishes it as physical also establishes the total collapse of the multi-timestep rate-coded correspondence of Section 7 to a single feedforward pass with no reset by subtraction, a collapse this survey traces as an algorithmic matter in Section 9.6. The route is scoped to BrainChip's standard converted-CNN execution and does not establish the semantics of every sequential, recurrent, or otherwise undocumented Akida mode. For the Akida 2.0 generation specifically, this survey's platform-capability record states the route state as unexercised rather than physical, because the same software chain establishes a generic Akida target without demonstrating a measured run on Akida 2.0 FPGA or production silicon specifically . Reaching physical silicon and preserving the contract fields a reader might assume a rate-coded conversion route preserves are, in this one chain, two separate and partially conflicting claims.

14.4 Blocked and Unexercised Routes

Two of the seven originally listed candidate routes are blocked at their first edge, and neither reaches the hardware target its name promises.

SpikingJelly → Lava → Loihi 2. SpikingJelly's default subtractive-reset conversion output, v_reset=None, is rejected by its own lava_exchange module before it ever reaches Lava-DL's NetX schema, exactly as Section 3.5 already establishes and Table 20 (rows 1 and 2) types edge by edge. This is a blocked route at the first edge for the default, published conversion output, not an unexercised or merely undocumented one; the exporter fails with a stated, deliberate error rather than silently miscompiling or simply lacking an example. A separately configured hard-zero model can enter the restricted downstream chain, lava_exchange to NetX's HDF5 schema to the Lava runtime, but that is a different model than the one the classical conversion recipe this survey has developed throughout Sections 7 through 9 actually produces, and no official example exercising that alternative, hard-zero-configured chain end to end to physical Loihi 2 was located in this survey's audited evidence . The downstream edges this alternative chain would use, lava_exchange to NetX and NetX to Loihi 2, are independently recorded as E3, documented but unexercised for this specific source contract, and the entire Lava, Lava-DL, and NetX stack's 2026 archival by Intel (Section 12.5) freezes whatever state each downstream edge held at the point of archival.

SpikingJelly → NIR → available backends. The same default reset configuration blocks this second candidate route at the same first edge for a related but distinct reason. SpikingJelly's nir_exchange module traces the model graph but raises NotImplementedError whenever v_reset is not set, because NIR 1.0.8's own IF, LIF, and CubaLIF primitives specify only a fixed-value reset and have no field capable of representing reset by subtraction at all . This is, in the terms Section 12.2 already uses, the format correctly rejecting a semantics it cannot express rather than a bug in SpikingJelly's own exporter. Once this first edge is blocked, the phrase available backends in the candidate route's name cannot be evaluated for SpikingJelly's own converted output, because there is no NIR graph for those backends to receive. NIR's fan-out to other frameworks remains real for other sources that do produce a representable graph, Sinabs to NIR is approximate and E2-evidenced through a physical Speck tutorial, and NIR to an official Lava or Lava-DL example is approximate through a documented but narrower zero-reset transformation, but neither of these is the SpikingJelly-authored chain the candidate route names, and NIR's own onward route to SpiNNaker 2 is independently blocked for the identical reset reason, at the identical first edge, before py-spinnaker2's importer is ever reached .

A further, more specific route belongs in this subsection precisely because its absence is evidentially informative rather than an oversight. This survey located no documented route, inside the public Lava or NetX toolchains or outside them, for implementing subtractive reset through custom Loihi 2 microcode. Loihi 2's neuron engine is documented as fully microcode-programmable, which makes impossibility an unwarranted inference from this absence . This survey accordingly records the edge as undocumented, a distinct route state from blocked, obsolete, or unexercised, and one that leaves the question open for a future audit rather than closing it in either direction.

14.5 Choosing a Route

The seven routes traced in this section do not rank neatly from best to worst, because the field each one preserves, transforms, or fails to reach at all is not the same field from route to route. A practitioner whose deployment claim depends specifically on reset by subtraction executing without a resource-doubling workaround has, in this survey's audited evidence, exactly three physical options, native subtraction on Speck through Sinabs, native subtraction on Xylo through Rockpool, or emulated subtraction on Loihi 1 through NxTF at the cost of a second compartment per neuron; SpiNNaker's two generations and the QuantizeML-to-Akida chain fail this specific requirement even though three of those four chains are otherwise physical. A practitioner whose claim depends only on reaching some physical target with a rate-coded, multi-step, feedforward convolutional network, without a further requirement on reset semantics, has a wider physical option set, but should read the QuantizeML-to-Akida route's own transformation of the timestep and reset fields as a warning against assuming that physical execution certifies the specific correspondence Section 7 derives. A practitioner drawn to SpikingJelly for its PyTorch-native training convenience should read this section's two blocked routes as a concrete cost of that convenience at the export boundary, not a permanent one, since a hard-zero-configured model can enter the restricted Lava-DL chain that a subtractive-reset model cannot, but a cost that changes what the exported network computes relative to the one actually trained.
