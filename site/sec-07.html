7 Direct SNN Training

This section explains direct training only as far as a deployment claim requires. The organizing question throughout is not which credit-assignment mechanism trains most accurately, but what each mechanism commits a resulting network to before that network reaches a target, and what the audited evidence in this survey actually shows about that commitment being exercised on physical hardware.

7.1 Local Learning and STDP

A local learning rule updates a synaptic weight \(w_{ij}^l\) from quantities already available at that synapse, typically the relative timing or recent history of the presynaptic and postsynaptic events \(z_j^{l-1}\) and \(z_i^l\), rather than from a loss gradient propagated backward through every later layer and step. Spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP), the best known instance, strengthens or weakens a synapse according to whether the presynaptic spike arrives shortly before or shortly after the postsynaptic one. The mechanism requires no global error signal and no unrolled trajectory of the kind Section 7.4 returns to, which is exactly what makes it attractive for a device that cannot afford to store one. Hebbian learning's 1949 postulate that co-active synapses strengthen together, and the contrastive variant of spike-driven synaptic plasticity, are earlier and related instances of the same local-update principle.

The audited deployment evidence for local learning is dominated by a narrower case than a general STDP training pipeline for an arbitrary task. Its strongest confirmed instances are hand-designed spiking circuits, in which the designer fixes the dynamics to solve one specific problem and plasticity, where present, adapts a bounded part of that fixed structure online rather than learning the structure itself. Imam and Cleland deploy an olfactory-bulb-model circuit that learns odor identity online through one-shot local plasticity on physical Loihi silicon of a generation the record does not specify, reaching E1 neuromorphic measurement. The Locally Competitive Algorithm, a sparse-coding circuit whose dynamics provably converge to a target optimum by construction rather than by training, reaches the same evidence class on physical Loihi 2 in the implementation by Parpart et al., and on Loihi, generation not given in the record, in the implementation by Tang et al.. A constraint-satisfaction solver built the same way reaches the same class on Loihi, generation likewise unspecified, in the implementation by Yakopcic et al.. Robotics contributes a further cluster at the same evidence class. Kreiser et al. report an iCub head-pose estimation circuit with on-chip plasticity on physical Loihi, a Kapoho Bay board, with the chip generation unspecified in the record. The NeuroPod hexapod central pattern generator and a goalkeeper robot's perception-to-action mapping from Cheng and Nikolić both run on SpiNNaker, generation unspecified in the record. A head-direction simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) circuit from Tang et al. runs on Loihi, generation likewise unspecified.

Local learning reaching hardware, in the evidence audited here, is largely a claim about hand-designed circuits with bounded local plasticity, not a demonstration that a general STDP training procedure reaches physical silicon for arbitrary tasks the way the gradient-based mechanisms in the next two subsections do. Absence of that broader demonstration in the audited corpus is not evidence that it is impossible, only that it was not found.

7.2 Surrogate Gradients and Backpropagation Through Time

A surrogate-gradient method keeps the exact binary forward pass \(z_i^l[t]=\mathcal{H}\!\left(\tilde{u}_i^l[t]-\theta_i^l[t]\right)\) fixed in Section 6.1 and changes only the backward pass, substituting a smooth surrogate derivative \(\sigma'(\cdot)\) for the Heaviside derivative at the same argument, \begin{equation}\label{eq:surrogate-derivative} \frac{\partial z_i^l[t]}{\partial \tilde{u}_i^l[t]} \;\approx\; \sigma'\!\left(\tilde{u}_i^l[t]-\theta_i^l[t]\right), \end{equation} so that automatic differentiation has a nonzero local sensitivity to propagate at every step. A directly trained SNN obtained this way is neuroscience-derived forward dynamics placed under deep-learning optimization, not a biologically faithful learning system in its own right; STDP in Section 7.1 is the branch that keeps the learning rule itself biological. Because the reset stage \(\mathcal{R}^l\) at step \(t\) determines the membrane state \(u_i^l[t]\) that stage \(\mathcal{M}^l\) reads at step \(t+1\), the network is unrolled across the full horizon \(T\) and treated as a deep, weight-tied recurrent graph, an application of backpropagation through time (BPTT) to the staged update of Section 3.2. The surrogate derivative is an approximation to the true one everywhere except exactly at the crossing point, so the resulting weight update is a descent direction for the surrogate loss landscape rather than an unbiased estimate of the gradient of the true, non-differentiable one. Backpropagating through this recurrence also means backpropagating through whichever reset rule, reset-to-zero or reset-by-subtraction, was assumed at Section 3.5, a choice a direct-training method must state rather than leave implicit, since it changes what the surrogate gradient is computed against.

Surrogate-gradient training is the mechanism behind most of this survey's audited direct-training deployments that reach physical hardware with an event-native workload and a vendor-supplied training-to-deployment pipeline. On event-based vision, Richter et al.'s own head-to-head comparison on physical Speck1 trains an integrate-and-fire convolutional network directly by BPTT and finds it reaches higher on-chip accuracy than an ANN-to-SNN converted baseline measured on the identical chip, 98.50 percent against 86.17 percent, at a higher energy cost per inference, 180 microjoules against 141 microjoules. Related BPTT-trained networks reach physical Speck and DYNAP-CNN silicon at E1 neuromorphic, and the Compositional Reconfigurable Inferencer (CRI) field-programmable gate array (FPGA) platform built at UC San Diego at E1 FPGA. On biosignal decoding, gradient-trained recurrent networks for electromyography (EMG) gesture classification and a graded-spike network for electroencephalography (EEG) seizure prediction are measured on physical Loihi and Loihi 2. On robotics, the mapless-navigation policy of Tang et al. and an industrial peg-in-hole force-control policy are trained end to end by gradient descent and measured on physical Loihi. Both robotics policies feed continuous sensor or proprioceptive state as a constant current to the first layer during training, the direct input convention already fixed in Section 4.3, rather than pre-encoding it into a spike train.

Speck1's own comparison, the only same-chip, head-to-head measurement of the two routes found in this survey's audited evidence, reports a specific accuracy advantage at a specific energy cost for one workload on one chip, not a general ranking; Section 7.4 returns to the memory cost this training method pays for that result.

7.3 Online and Hardware-in-the-Loop Learning

Two further mechanisms respond to the surrogate-gradient family differently rather than replacing it outright. Exact spike-time gradient methods, including EXODUS and EventProp, differentiate analytically through the timing of the spike-generation event itself rather than substituting a surrogate slope for the same crossing, an alternative formulation rather than an approximation to the mechanism in Section 7.2. SLAYER is often filed beside them but belongs to the surrogate family, since it backpropagates through a stochastic spike-escape-rate approximation of the threshold crossing. A SLAYER-trained convolutional network reaches physical Loihi 1 through the NxTF compiler for dynamic vision sensor (DVS) Gesture classification, with latency and energy measured on-chip. EventProp, trained through mlGeNN and exported through NetX, deploys a leaky integrate-and-fire network with learned synaptic delays to physical single-chip Loihi 2 for keyword spotting on the Spiking Heidelberg Digits and Spiking Speech Commands benchmarks, reported eighteen times faster and two hundred fifty times lower in energy than an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano baseline at batch size one, both at E1 neuromorphic.

Hardware-in-the-loop (HITL) training is the most literal sense of online learning in the audited evidence, because the forward pass executes on the physical target device at every training iteration rather than on a simulator standing in for it, and only the backward pass runs on a host, using either an approximation to the device's true derivative or a measured trace injected into automatic differentiation. On BrainScaleS-1, a naive rate-coded transfer of an MNIST classifier without HITL correction collapses accuracy from 97 percent in software to 72 percent on the physical wafer-scale chip, recovering only to 95 percent, still short of the software figure, once the HITL correction is applied. On BrainScaleS-2, hxtorch.snn inserts measured membrane traces sampled from the physical chip directly into PyTorch's automatic-differentiation graph and trains with surrogate gradients computed from that injected physical trace rather than from a simulated one; the published Yin-Yang classification demonstration reports 94.63 plus or minus 0.7 percent accuracy over fifteen seeds on physical silicon, an E2 neuromorphic claim scoped to that one demonstration rather than a general capability statement about the platform.

Both mechanisms trade one cost for another rather than eliminating cost outright. Exact spike-time gradients still require the full trajectory needed to evaluate the analytic spike-time derivative, so they inherit the memory profile Section 7.4 describes for BPTT despite avoiding the surrogate approximation. Hardware-in-the-loop training substitutes a physical forward pass for a simulated one, at the cost of a hardware-specific backward model or an instrumented physical readout, engineering built for one target that does not transfer to a different chip without being rebuilt.

7.4 Training Cost, State Unrolling, and Memory

Because the reset stage \(\mathcal{R}^l\) at step \(t\) determines the membrane state \(u_i^l[t]\) that stage \(\mathcal{M}^l\) reads at step \(t+1\), computing a gradient at any step depends on every earlier step through this recurrence, exactly as in a recurrent neural network. Backpropagating through that dependency chain, whether by the surrogate derivative of Section 7.2 or the exact spike-time derivative of Section 7.3, requires retaining every intermediate pre-reset and post-reset state across the whole horizon \(T\) before the backward pass can run. Training memory therefore grows with \(T\) and network depth jointly, unlike the single, memoryless evaluation of an ANN unit fixed in Section 2.2.

This is the reason truncated backpropagation windows, checkpointing, and gradient approximations recur across the direct-training literature, and it is also the reason local and eligibility-trace mechanisms trade exactness for a memory profile that does not grow with \(T\). The eligibility-trace formulation of Bellec et al., already used in Section 3.3 for the adaptive-threshold neuron, keeps one running per-synapse trace instead of the full trajectory, at the cost of approximating the credit a distant past step should receive rather than computing it exactly. 7.5 Hardware-Aware and On-Chip Learning

A hardware-aware training procedure incorporates a specific deployment target's numeric or temporal constraints into the objective or the gradient computation itself, rather than training a target-agnostic network and relying on a later mapping step to preserve its behavior. Arfa et al. combine hardware-aware fine-tuning with eight-bit quantization to deploy spiking Q-networks, reinforcement-learning policies rather than classifiers, to physical SpiNNaker2, preserving a rate-coded, subtract-reset network evaluated over ten or twenty ticks and reaching E1 neuromorphic measurement. This is direct Q-learning with surrogate-gradient fine-tuning for one target, not ANN-to-SNN conversion.

On-chip learning is a narrower and distinct claim from hardware-aware training. It means the weight update itself executes on the neuromorphic device, rather than on a host machine that trains offline and later downloads a fixed weight set for inference. The capability vocabulary fixed for the rest of this survey applies here too, native, transformed, emulated, host-assisted, unsupported, and undocumented. Undocumented is its own distinct status and carries no inference either way, the status of custom, non-public Loihi 2 microcode for subtractive reset outside the public Lava and NetX toolchains (Section 11.5). 7.6 What Direct Training Fixes for Deployment

Across every mechanism audited in this section, from hand-designed local plasticity to hardware-in-the-loop gradients, direct training's defining deployment property recurs. The neuron dynamics, code, precision, and timing model used during optimization are, in the audited evidence, generally the ones executed at inference, because the gradient or the local rule is computed on that exact representation rather than on a separate proxy. A directly trained network's parameters are consequently often tied to the specific assumptions used to obtain them, a platform's particular reset rule, its exact quantization, its exact timing model, more tightly than a converted network's are, since conversion has not yet made those choices at the point its ANN is trained, the asymmetry Section 6.3 already previewed. This tying is a property of how a network was obtained, not a claim that direct training cannot produce a portable one. What it contributes to the deployable contract of Section 9 is a network whose neuron model, code, and precision fields are typically settled jointly with its weights, at the cost of the training-time memory and hardware-specific engineering of Section 7.4 and Section 7.5; Section 8 inverts the order, choosing them only afterward.
