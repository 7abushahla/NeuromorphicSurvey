9 The Deployable SNN Contract

Section 8 closed by observing that every conversion method it reviewed reports its result against an implicit agreement about which fields of the network a comparison holds fixed, code, precision, reset, time model, and target hardware, and which it silently lets vary. Nothing in Sections 6 through 8 has forced that agreement to be written down. This section writes it down. It fixes eleven fields that a directly trained network and a converted network must both make explicit before either is compared against a target, or against each other, at all. The five terms Section 8 named are not a shorter, competing list. Code and precision name two of the eleven fields directly, reset and time model name two more under fuller names, and target hardware names the object every field is evaluated against rather than a twelfth field of the network itself.

9.1 Why a Shared Contract

Section 6.3 already stated the requirement this section formalizes. Whichever route produces the weights, direct training by the mechanisms of Section 7 or conversion by the derivation of Section 8, the result is a spiking network of the form fixed in Section 3, carrying a code of the form fixed in Section 4. It must be representable in software and executable on a target before a deployment claim about it means anything. What was missing at that point was not a new fact about either branch. It was a single, named list of the semantic commitments both branches make, so that a reader can ask the same question of a paper regardless of which branch produced its network.

The contract answers three separate questions for each of its eleven fields, and this survey keeps the three separate throughout the remainder of the article. Is the field mathematically defined? A neuron equation, a reset rule, or a code can be written down precisely, as Section 3 and Section 4 do, independent of any software or hardware that will later realize it. Can a software framework represent it? A training or simulation framework, an interchange format, or a compiler must accept the mathematical object without silently rewriting its meaning, the question Section 10 answers edge by edge. Can the target execute it without changing what the network computes? A chip, accelerator, or emulator must run the represented object under its own timing, precision, and routing constraints, the question Section 5 and Section 11 answer per platform and per named route. A field can pass one question and fail the next. Section 3.5 already gave the clearest instance available in this survey's audited evidence. Reset by subtraction is mathematically defined without difficulty. It is not representable in the NIR 1.0.8 schema, which encodes only a fixed-value post-spike reset . Where it is executed at all on Intel Loihi 1, it is emulated with a second compartment rather than run on a native single-compartment primitive . The same field is defined, unrepresentable in one interchange format, and emulated on one platform, three different answers for one field.

Two properties of Table 21 recur throughout the rest of this article and are stated once here rather than at every field. Second, the eleven fields are not independent in the sense that satisfying one guarantees anything about another. 9.3 Definition, Representation, and Execution

The three questions introduced in Section 9.1 are not stages of one pipeline that a field passes through in order. They are independent tests, and a field can fail any one of them while passing the other two. Restating them against the reset example makes the independence concrete rather than abstract.

Mathematical definition asks whether an equation exists. Section 3.5 writes reset by subtraction as \(u_i^l[t]=\tilde{u}_i^l[t]-z_i^l[t]\,\theta_i^l[t]\), a complete, unambiguous definition that requires no software or hardware to state. Software representation asks whether a framework, an interchange format, or a compiler can accept that definition and hand it downstream unchanged. SpikingJelly 2.0.0rc1's own rate-coding conversion recipe instantiates exactly this reset rule by default, so the framework that produces the network represents the field correctly at the point of production . The same network's own NIR and Lava exporters both reject that default output outright, because neither downstream schema has a field for anything other than a fixed-value reset . The field is representable at the point where the network is defined and unrepresentable one export step later, inside the same toolchain. Faithful execution asks whether a target that does accept some representation of the field runs it as specified. NxTF's Loihi 1 pipeline recovers the mathematical behavior of subtractive reset, but only by allocating a second compartment per affected neuron and using a recurrent inhibition trick to reproduce the subtraction, an emulated rather than a native capability with an explicit resource cost . One field, one clean mathematical definition, three different answers depending on which of the three questions is asked and at which boundary of the pipeline it is asked.

This three-way independence is the reason a single adjective, portable, supported, or compatible, is not an adequate description of any field in this contract. Section 10 develops the representation question into a typed schema applied to every audited software boundary. Section 5 develops the execution question into a comparison across execution families, and Section 9.5 into a capability matrix scoped to specific, versioned platforms. Section 11 then composes both answers across a complete chain of edges, from a training framework to a physical target, and assigns the resulting route a single validated state precisely because no earlier section in this survey has assumed that a route's state can be read off any one boundary in isolation.

9.4 Conformance and the Capability Vocabulary

Evaluating a field against Table 21 requires evidence of three specific kinds, and a field is triangulated only when at least the software and platform kinds can be checked against each other. Algorithm sources are the papers that propose a training method or a conversion algorithm and state, explicitly or by omission, what their own network assumes about each field. Software sources are the documented behavior of a training framework, an interchange format, or a compiler, checked wherever possible against the framework's own source code rather than its prose description alone, exactly as Section 3.5 did for SpikingJelly's exporters. Official platform sources are versioned vendor manuals, SDK and development-kit documentation, mapper and compiler release notes, and official worked examples, the same evidentiary standard this survey's platform-capability records apply throughout Sections 5 and 11.

A field's status against a target is recorded using the capability vocabulary this survey fixes in Section 7.5, native, transformed, emulated, host-assisted, unsupported, and undocumented . These labels answer a different question than the route-state vocabulary, exact, approximate, blocked, obsolete, unexercised, and physical, which Section 11 assigns to a complete chain of edges rather than to one field. A field can be natively supported on a chip whose overall route to that chip from a specific training framework remains blocked at an earlier edge, exactly the case Section 9.5 and Section 11 document for Loihi 2's threshold and reset field under the audited SpikingJelly-to-Lava chain. Section 7.5 also fixes undocumented as its own distinct status carrying no inference either way, using the same Loihi 2 custom-microcode gap this survey traces for reset by subtraction.

Every platform-capability record this survey draws on for Sections 5 and 11 carries a verification status of its own, separate from the capability status it reports. At the time of the evidence audit underlying this survey, every one of the nine platform-capability records assembled for the detailed comparison in Section 9.5 remains provisional, because the canonical vendor manual behind each record is registered in this survey's source registry as reviewed from structured metadata or partial excerpts rather than promoted to a fully inspected, stably located full-text source. A physically exercised route can therefore coexist with a provisional field-level record, when the official technical source documenting that field has not itself been promoted, or when the exercised example uses a different upstream route than the one a specific field claim describes. This survey states that limitation once here rather than qualifying every cell of the tables that follow, and the distinction between a physically exercised route and a fully promoted documentary source remains active throughout Section 5 and Section 11.

The representation question comes first in every remaining section of this survey, because a field that cannot survive a software boundary never reaches a target for the execution question to be asked of it at all. Section 10 takes up that question next, following the software path from a training framework through serialization, an intermediate representation, compilation, and a runtime, and applying the input, output, preserved, transformed, rejected, and evidence schema to every edge the audited evidence supports.

Reading a platform's row in isolation, without checking which fields the specific audited route actually exercises, risks exactly the overclaim this survey's capability vocabulary exists to prevent.

A platform's contract-field status, however complete, describes what that platform can do in principle for one audited configuration. It does not, on its own, establish that a specific published network traveled the complete chain from a training framework to that platform without breaking at an intermediate edge. Section 11 closes that gap, tracing seven named candidate routes onto vendor silicon, six neuromorphic chips and Akida's event NPU, and, in Section 11.3, Routes to Conventional Targets, five onto conventional processors, edge by edge through exactly the software boundaries Section 10 types and exactly the hardware families this section compares, and assigning each one a single validated route state rather than inferring it from how compatible the endpoints look on paper.
