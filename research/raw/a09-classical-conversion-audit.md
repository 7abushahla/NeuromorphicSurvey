# A09 — Deployment Audit of the Classical ANN-to-SNN Conversion Literature

Audit question for every paper: **did it run on hardware, and if so what hardware, and what was measured?**

Classification key:
- **E1** — physical silicon, latency and/or energy measured on-chip
- **E2** — physical silicon, accuracy only measured on-chip
- **E3** — documented hardware-deployment path, but no run reported
- **E4** — hardware model (RTL / cycle-accurate simulator / analytical energy model), not physical silicon
- **E5** — software simulation only (GPU/CPU), including SynOps-based energy *estimates* — these are estimates, not measurements

**Summary for Part 1 (the 9 core algorithm-proposing classical conversion papers named in the audit brief): 0 of 9 report physical hardware execution. All 9 are E5.** Several (Cao 2015, Sengupta 2019, Han & Roy 2020 TSC, Li et al. 2021) report analytical/SynOps-style energy or operation-count *estimates* that are frequently miscited as "hardware results" in downstream literature; none of these are chip measurements.

## Notes on scope and borderline classifications

1. **Esser et al. 2016 (TrueNorth/Eedn) is frequently cited in surveys as an "ANN-to-SNN conversion" success story reaching hardware.** On inspection of the method, it is not threshold-balancing/weight-transplant conversion at all — it is direct, hardware-constrained backpropagation training (binary neurons with surrogate gradients, trinary synapses) designed from the outset to map onto TrueNorth. It belongs in the neighboring literature on hardware-aware/constrained training, not in the classical conversion lineage of Cao/Diehl/Rueckauer/Sengupta. It is included here because the audit brief explicitly asked for it to be checked, but its E1 status should not be read as "classical conversion reached hardware" — it demonstrates something different (co-designed training reaches hardware).

2. **Stromatias et al. 2015 (SpiNNaker)** is conversion-*adjacent*: a Deep Belief Network trained offline is realized as a spiking network on SpiNNaker. It predates and is architecturally distinct from the CNN-specific IF-neuron threshold-balancing pipeline that Diehl (2015) and Rueckauer (2017) formalized. It is the earliest true E1 result in the neighborhood of this literature.

3. **No paper in Part 1 (the algorithm-proposing papers) reports any physical hardware execution.** Every accuracy/latency/energy number in Cao 2015, Diehl 2015, Rueckauer 2017, Sengupta 2019, Han 2020 (RMP-SNN and TSC), Deng & Gu 2021, Li et al. 2021, and Ho & Chang 2021 comes from a software (GPU/CPU) simulator. Several of these papers report SynOps-based or per-operation analytical energy/complexity *estimates* (Cao 2015's "hardware mapping analysis," Sengupta 2019's "reduced hardware overhead" claim, Han & Roy 2020's operation-count reduction, Li et al. 2021's explicit 0.9 pJ/4.6 pJ per-operation energy formula) — these are estimates, not measurements, and are classified E5 per the audit's explicit instruction that SynOps-based estimates are not hardware measurements.

4. **The Rueckauer et al. 2017 SNN Toolbox is the pivot point of the literature**: the algorithm paper itself never runs on hardware (E5), but the toolbox it produced becomes, years later, the conversion front-end for at least three independent hardware-deployment papers (Massa et al. 2020 on Loihi, Rueckauer et al. 2021 NxTF on Loihi, and implicitly the broader SNN Toolbox → SpiNNaker/Loihi backend support referenced in its own GitHub documentation). Hardware validation of "classical conversion" as a methodology exists, but it lives entirely in downstream systems/compiler papers, not in the original algorithmic contributions.

5. **Scale gap on hardware.** Every E1 result in Part 2 is either a small/shallow network (LeNet-scale MNIST/N-MNIST classifiers, small DVS-gesture CNNs, DBNs) or, for Esser et al. 2016 (a non-conversion outlier), a network co-designed for the hardware from the start. No classical threshold-balancing conversion of a full VGG-16 or ResNet-34 trained the way Sengupta (2019), Han (2020), or Deng & Gu (2021) do has been demonstrated running on physical neuromorphic silicon in the papers surveyed here — the CIFAR-10 8.52% figure on Loihi (Rueckauer et al. 2021) uses MobileNet, not VGG-16/ResNet, and ImageNet-scale conversion has not been shown on physical hardware in any paper found in this audit.

## Summary statistic

- **Classical conversion papers audited (Part 1, algorithm-proposing): 9**
  - E1/E2 (physical silicon, any measurement): **0 / 9 (0%)**
  - E4/E5 (simulation or analytical estimate only): **9 / 9 (100%)**, all specifically E5

- **Hardware-reaching papers identified in the neighboring deployment/systems literature (Part 2): 5**
  - Esser et al. 2016 (TrueNorth) — E1, but methodologically direct-trained, not classical conversion
  - Stromatias et al. 2015 (SpiNNaker) — E1, DBN-conversion-adjacent
  - Massa et al. 2020 (Loihi, DVS Gesture) — E1, uses SNN Toolbox conversion
  - Rueckauer et al. 2021 NxTF (Loihi) — E1, uses SNN Toolbox conversion
  - Kelber et al. 2020 (BrainScaleS/Spikey/SpiNNaker) — E1 (digital platforms), E5 (BrainScaleS energy figure specifically, estimated not measured)

- **Combined (all 14 papers audited): 5 / 14 (36%) reached E1; 9 / 14 (64%) are E4/E5.** But restricting to the papers that actually *propose* a classical conversion algorithm (Part 1), the figure is a clean **0%** — hardware validation of classical ANN-to-SNN conversion methods happens exclusively in separate, later, systems-oriented papers that adopt someone else's conversion pipeline as an input, never in the papers that introduce the conversion technique itself.
